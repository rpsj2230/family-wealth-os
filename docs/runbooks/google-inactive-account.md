# Google Inactive Account Manager: telling your family if your Google account goes quiet

The family portal reads your bank and fund e-mails from Gmail. If something happens to you, Google can tell your wife (or anyone you
choose) automatically, and share chosen parts of your account with her. It is free and set up once, in your Google account. The portal's
own inactivity alert (Settings > Access) is the quick one, after weeks; Google's is the backstop, after months.

## What Google counts as "active"

Google treats the account as in use when it sees any of these ([Google's help page](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/3036546)):

- **Signing in** to your Google account, on any device or browser.
- **Activity recorded in My Activity**: for example searches, YouTube or Maps while signed in.
- **Gmail use**, including the Gmail app on your phone opening or syncing your mailbox.
- **Android check-ins**: an Android phone signed in to your account and switched on counts, even if you do nothing.

So an Android phone that stays switched on and signed in, or a Gmail app that keeps syncing, keeps the account "active". Keep that in
mind: if your phone stays on (for example kept charged by someone at home), Google may not see the account as inactive. The portal's
own alert is different: it counts only you (or another member) signing in to the family portal.

## The waiting period: 15 days is not possible

Google's shortest waiting period is **3 months**; you cannot choose days. The choices offered are 3, 6, 12 or 18 months (the list has
changed over the years; the page shows the current ones). Choose **3 months**. The 15-to-30-day window is covered by the portal's own
alert instead.

## Setting it up (about 10 minutes)

1. On a computer, sign in to the Google account the portal reads (the Gmail with your bank and fund e-mails).
2. Open **[myaccount.google.com/inactive](https://myaccount.google.com/inactive)** and press **Start**.
3. **When to consider the account inactive**: choose **3 months**. Check your phone number and recovery e-mail. Google sends its
   reminders there before it does anything, so they must be ones you use.
4. **Choose who to notify and what to share**: add your wife (and, if you wish, a trusted adult or your CA), up to 10 people.
   - Enter her e-mail address and her mobile number. Google uses the number to check it is really her before she can download anything.
   - Tick what to share with her. Recommended: **Gmail** (the bank and fund e-mails, statements and password-protected PDFs arrive
     there) and **Drive** (if you keep documents there). You can give different people different things.
   - Write the message she receives, for example:
     > "If you are reading this, I have not used my Google account for three months. Our family's money is written down in a private
     > portal: https://family-wealth-os-nine.vercel.app. Sign in with the family login; the password is kept in [where]. The handbook
     > there tells you what to do, step by step. Call [name] first."
     Write where the password is kept, never the password itself.
5. **Optional**: an automatic reply from Gmail to anyone who writes to you after the account is inactive. Usually leave it off.
6. **Optional**: whether Google deletes the account afterwards. Choose **No**: your family may need the e-mails for months.
7. Press **Review plan** and **Confirm**. Google sends you a confirmation e-mail.

Your contacts are not told anything now. They hear from Google only after the waiting period, and Google first reminds you by text
message and e-mail before it acts, so a long holiday away from your accounts will not set it off without warning.

## Keep it current

- Check the plan once a year (the portal can remind you): the phone numbers, e-mails and the portal address in the message.
- If you change your phone number or recovery e-mail, update them in your Google account the same day.
