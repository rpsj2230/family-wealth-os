# Family Wealth OS: user guide

For Rupash and Ragini. Everything here describes what the app does today. Items marked _(waiting)_ need something from you first (see `docs/NEEDS-FROM-YOU.md`).

## What the numbers mean

- **Verified** is the last balance you entered or an official statement showed, with its date.
- **Estimated today** is the verified figure moved forward: fixed deposits by their own interest terms, savings at an assumed rate, mutual funds by the latest published NAV, Singapore dollars by the latest exchange rate. Each figure is labelled _calculated_, _estimated_ or _carried forward_.
- An asset with no value is listed but never counted as zero. Insurance cover is never counted in net worth, and nor is a deposit marked as matured and unconfirmed. **A deposit counts until its maturity date, and a deposit maturing today still counts until tomorrow.** From the next day its money is no longer in the deposit: it went into a savings account or a new deposit, which are counted on their own. So a deposit whose date has passed is left out of net worth, the deposit and bank figures, "I need money", the insured-limit view, the reports, goals and every other total, whatever its status says; it stays on the lists, muted and marked "matured, not counted", until you press **Confirm matured**, and Home notes the amount under the banks card.
- Money you have lent is shown as owed, not as cash.

## The ? next to a figure

A small round **?** sits next to the headline figures on Home, Accounts, Deposits, Cash, Investments (and a fund's page), Reports, Tax, the Monthly update, New money and Rates. Press it (or Tab to it and press Enter or Space) and a card opens; on a phone it slides up from the bottom. Escape, the **X**, or a tap outside closes it and you are back where you were.

The card says, in this order: **what the figure is** in one sentence; **how it is worked out**, with the real numbers put in and the base of every percentage ("₹8,03,737 profit ÷ ₹43,01,000 money put in = +18.7%"; the same profit as a share of today's value is a different percentage, and the card says which is which); **where it comes from**, one line per input with its date range and a word saying what kind of data it is; and **how complete it is**, with the funds or accounts that hold it back and a link to fix them (the Investments data check, the Import page, the Monthly update). Where a number is shown rounded (₹8.04 L) the card also gives the exact rupees, and so does hovering over it.

Every input is one of five kinds, each shown as a word as well as a colour:

- **statement**: read from a fund statement, a bank statement or a document.
- **recorded**: typed in or confirmed by a person (a balance, a unit count, a deposit's terms, a setting).
- **calculated**: worked out exactly from recorded facts (units times a price, a deposit's interest from its own terms).
- **estimated**: worked out from a rate, a plan or a price move; a good guess, not a record.
- **missing**: the data is not there, so the figure is left out or reduced; the card says what is needed.

The whole figure is then **Complete**, **Partly estimated**, **Estimated** or **Missing data**. This is the same judgement the "est." mark, the fund history check and the "older than expected" words already make; the card only explains it.

## Home

The first page answers, from the top down:

1. **What are we worth?** The big number is net worth in lakhs and crores with the exact rupees under it. Beside it, **How much is verified** shows the share of that value that was recorded recently and confirmed, or worked out from a deposit's own terms (the rest is older, or imported and not yet confirmed). Beneath the number are three figures, each with an arrow and a word: **Today** (an estimate: interest earned since yesterday at each rate plus the move in fund prices), the **last 30 days** and **this financial year** (from 1 April). The last two are what your holdings **earned** in that time (interest, fund price moves, exchange-rate moves), not the change in net worth, so money you added or took out is never in them. Each says where its period really begins ("since 28 Sep 2026", "since 1 Apr 2026") and how many of your holdings it rests on ("86 of 100 holdings had a value then"), and under it, in smaller type, how far net worth itself moved (money added or taken out included). Only when there is no record at all before the start of a period does it say "data starts 3 Mar 2025". The 30 days is a setting.
   Under the net worth are five **money figures**: **Total invested** (the money in deposits and funds: each deposit's amount and the cost of the fund units still held; savings and cash balances, shares, property and money lent are not "invested"), **Current value** (the same net worth), **Maturity value** (deposits at what they pay on their dates, everything else at today's value), **Profit so far** (what the deposits and funds are worth above that invested money: interest accrued on deposits plus the gain on the fund units held, in rupees and as a share of the money invested at cost; the tile says what the invested money is worth now) and **Earned today**. A mark **est.** (hover for the reason) is on every figure that rests on an estimate: interest at a rate, a fund price move, a plan. It is the same mark on every page.

   Next come **four small cards**, one per kind of holding (**Fixed deposits**, **Mutual funds**, **Cash**, **Others**), that add up to the net worth. Each shows what is worth now, what was **invested**, what it has **earned so far** and the **profit so far** with an arrow and a word, and its share of the total; each links to its own page. Fixed deposits: invested is the amount of each deposit and the value is its accrued value today. Mutual funds: invested is the money still in the funds (the cost of the units held, as Investments shows it) and the value is units times the latest price, with SIP instalments assumed since the last statement. Cash: invested is the last balance recorded and the profit is the interest estimated since, at the account's own rate, else the Rates list, else the assumed rate in Settings, after the tax taken at source. Others (shares, property, money lent, held by others, pension accounts): shown at the value you recorded, with no profit unless a recorded value has moved on from what was recorded. **Earned so far** also counts interest from deposits already paid out and gains from units already sold, so it can be more than the profit on what you still hold. **How these are worked out** under the cards says all of this.

   **Cash in another currency.** The **Cash** card's big figure is always all your cash in rupees: the rupee accounts plus every foreign-currency account (for example a Singapore dollar savings account) converted at today's exchange rate, exactly as the net worth counts them. Under it, in small type, each foreign currency is listed on its own: the balance in its own money (`S$ 41,250`, or `AED 12,000` with the three-letter code when the app has no symbol for the currency) and below it what that is worth in rupees with the rate and its day (`≈ ₹23.14 L at 1 SGD = ₹56.1, rate of 26 Oct`). One line per currency, the biggest in rupees first; with no foreign cash nothing extra appears. The rate is the latest exchange rate on file (refreshed every day from a public source), which is the rate behind the rupee value in the net worth; if none is on file the rate saved with the balance is used and the line says "rate recorded 30 Sep". The rate shown is the rupee value divided by the balance. A move in the rate changes the rupee value, not the balance, and is not counted as interest. The same lines appear on the **Savings and cash** figure on Accounts, under the total on Cash, in small type under each bank's total and each person's savings on Accounts (`incl. S$ 41,250 ≈ ₹23.14 L`), and under the Savings tile in the daily summary e-mail, its plain text and the WhatsApp message. The **?** next to these figures says how the rate was chosen.

   **Profit earned, by period** is a table with a row for each kind and the total, and a column for the last 30 days, this month, this quarter, this financial year (from 1 April), last financial year and since the start. It is worked out from the terms of each holding, not from the saved net worth, so money you added or took out is never counted as profit: a new deposit or fund purchase, a payout or a sale is shown beneath as "put in" or "paid out". Deposits: the interest accrued in the period on each deposit's own terms (a deposit paid out in the period counts its interest up to that day). Mutual funds: the change in value over the period less the money put in; a gain already booked by selling counts in the period it was sold. Cash: interest on the recorded balance at the account's rate after tax at source; a foreign-currency balance earns nothing here. Others: no profit. The per cent is of the money at work (the value at the start plus new money for the days it was there); since the start is against all the money put in. The length of "last 30 days" is the same setting as the change at the top. On a phone each kind is its own card.

   The same period gives the same rupees elsewhere: earned today on Accounts, Deposits and Cash, interest in each financial year in Reports and the period figures and profit-and-loss on Investments are worked out by the same rules (one module), and tests check they agree.
2. **Needs you** lists what needs a person, late things first (matured deposits to confirm, loans past their date, overdue tasks and policy dates), then things with a date coming (deposits maturing soon, tasks and policies due, money above the deposit-insurance limit, bank e-mails to review), then housekeeping (balances out of date, holdings with no value). Every line says how many and links to where it is dealt with.
3. **Coming up** puts deposits (grouped by day), policy dates, tasks, repayments and the end of the financial year on one timeline. How far ahead it looks is a setting (120 days).
4. **What changed** shows the biggest moves since the first day of the last 30 days, by kind of holding and by person, with the position on that day rebuilt from the records on file. A move includes money you added, a deposit that matured and interest and price changes.
5. **Net worth over time**: choose 1M, 3M, 6M, 1Y or All (each is a link, so the back button works). Months before the app was in use are points **rebuilt from history** (see below) and the note under the chart says how many. It shows the change over that stretch and over the same length of time before it, a dotted line for "if the last year's pace continues" (a straight extension, not a forecast), and, for banks and funds, how much was put in against how much it has grown. Property, shares and money lent have no recorded cost and are left out of that split.
6. **Where the money is** by kind of holding, by person (each name links to Accounts for that person), and by NRE or NRO and currency when you have more than one.
7. **How fast can I get at it?** First what arrives with nothing sold (accounts now, then each deposit as it matures), then, for each window set under "When I need money", the most that could be in the bank at no cost and with sales and early breaks included, before tax and penalties. **I need money** shows the cost of each way.
8. **In banks**, **Fixed deposits** and **Mutual funds** are short versions of Accounts, Deposits and Investments and use the same figures. Deposits also show the interest added this financial year and what the running deposits add over the next 12 months if held to maturity. Funds show value, gain, annual return (XIRR), today's move, best and weakest, and a note on which funds have a statement on file (**est.** marks a return worked out from the plan you entered).
9. **Goals** and **What to watch** (deposit insurance, the largest bank, the largest kind of holding, late loans).

**How these figures are worked out** (under the top box) explains each figure and lists the latest recorded values. In Settings > Money rules > Reminders you can change what "this month" means, how far ahead Coming up looks, and the share at which a bank is flagged.

## How a period figure is worked out

Every figure for a stretch of time (the last 30 days, this month, quarter or financial year, last financial year, since the start; Home, the daily summary e-mail and the WhatsApp text) comes from one function, and the same rule is used for the first day and the last day: **each holding is valued on that day from the nearest record on or before it, moved forward to the day with what is known about it.** If you recorded a balance on 22 Aug and nothing on 28 Sep, the first day of the last 30 days is worked out from the 22 Aug balance at the account's rate; it does not start at "the first record". The "?" on each figure names the records it rests on ("records from 22 Aug to 26 Sep"). Only when nothing at all is on record before the first day does the period begin where the data does and say "data starts ...".

| Kind of holding | Its value on a day D | What it earned in a period | Never counted as earning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Savings and cash (rupees) | The last balance recorded on or before D, plus interest from that day to D at the account's rate (its own, else the Rates list for its bank, kind and balance slab, else the assumed rate in Settings), after the tax taken at source. Marked est. unless it is the record of that day. | The interest that balance earns in the period at that rate. | A deposit into or a withdrawal from the account, a balance that jumped because of a transfer, interest the bank credited in a lump (the balance you record then replaces the estimate). |
| Fixed deposit | From its own terms: principal grown to D on its compounding, at the rate that takes it to the bank's maturity amount when that is on file (the page says "from the bank's maturity amount"), else the rate on the deposit. Held from its booking day until the day it was paid out or closed, else until its maturity date; after that it is not counted. | `value at the end − value at the start` for deposits held through the period; interest up to the day it left for one paid out; interest from its booking day for a new one. | The principal of a new deposit (even when it came out of savings) and the payout of one that matured. |
| Mutual fund | **Units held on D times the price of D**, always. The units come, in this order, from: (1) the fund's transactions (a fund statement, then an app export), with the units held before the first line; (2) with no transactions, **your plan**: the fund's start date, the amount invested, and the SIP amount with every change and stop date. Each instalment buys its amount divided by the price of its day (the next priced day when it fell on a weekend or holiday); if the amount invested is more than the plan explains, the rest is an opening purchase on the start date, and if it is less, the instalments are scaled down to fit it (marked est.; a unit count you typed in replaces the plan's up to its day); (3) only for a fund with no start date or no price: the newest statement, recorded or monthly-sheet value on or before D moved with the price (est., "last resort"). Today uses the latest price. | Value at the end − value at the start − purchases + sales (for a fund from the plan, the plan's instalments are the purchases). | Money put in or taken out (a purchase, a SIP instalment, a redemption). A gain already booked by a sale counts in the period it was sold in. Between two monthly sheet values a fund is moved with its price; money added by the SIP plan and any difference between the sheet and the prices is not counted as profit (it is shown apart: "N funds without a statement: ₹X of change between sheet values is not counted as profit"). |
| Foreign balance or holding | The amount in its own currency last recorded on or before D times that currency's exchange rate on D (est.). | The move of the exchange rate on the amount held. | A new deposit or withdrawal in that currency. |
| Shares, property, pension accounts, money lent, held by others | The last value recorded on or before D, held flat. | Nothing, unless Settings > Estimated balances gives a yearly growth for that kind; then that growth, marked "assumed". | A new record (money added, a repayment, a revaluation you typed in). |
| Insurance cover | Never wealth. | | |

**Fund gains are approximate until you import your fund statements.** While some funds have no transaction ledger, a small line under Home's period tiles says how many ("3 of 26 funds have no transaction ledger") and links to Import > Fund statement; it goes when a statement has been imported for all of them. Settings > Funds > "Warn about approximate fund gains while this many funds lack a transaction ledger" (default 0: any) and "A fund's SIP plan may differ from the amount invested by" (default 2%) are the two numbers behind it. A fund's value is the same on Home, Investments (Funds, Value by period), the daily e-mail and the WhatsApp text, because all of them use the same function.

**Total invested and profit.** Total invested is the money put into deposits and funds (deposit principal plus the cost of the fund units still held); savings and cash balances, shares, property and money lent are in net worth but are not invested money. Profit so far is what those deposits and funds are worth above it, and the per cent is of the money invested, at cost. "Since the start" in the table also counts interest already paid out and gains already booked by selling.

**Whether the records are enough.** The settings "Flag a savings account whose records grow this far from its rate" (default 2 points a year) and "Rebuilt history agrees with the workbook within" (1%) are on Settings > Estimated balances. An account whose recorded balances grow at a rate far from the one it is valued at is marked "earning about X% a year (from your records)": money you added is in those balances, so this is a pointer and never changes a figure.

## Monthly routine (about 5 minutes)

1. Open **Monthly update**. The top box says which banks are up to date for the month, which are waiting for a value, and how each latest balance arrived (email, you, a statement, the imported sheet or a calculation).
2. Work down the banks that are waiting. Type the new balance and press Enter for the next one, or paste a column from Excel or your bank app into the first box. **Same** keeps one balance as it was; **Nothing changed at (bank)** does it for the whole bank. A note under a box tells you how far the new figure is from the last, and warns about a likely typing slip.
3. To change a rate, a note, a name or a deposit's terms while you are here, press **Edit** on that line (the same panel as on Accounts; see "Changing one account" below). Press **Save**. Nothing is stored until then, and **Discard** drops the lot. The dashboard and the history chart update.
4. Deposits are worked out from their terms and sit in a closed box under each bank; only a matured deposit needs you to say where the money went. Property, pension, shares and money lent are on the second tab, **Property, loans and other**.
5. Check **Bank emails** for balances read from your email that are waiting for you, and **Tasks** for anything the app has raised.

## Bank emails

Forward bank alerts to the Gmail label shown in Settings. A balance from the bank's own address that matches exactly one of your accounts is recorded by itself. Everything else waits under **Needs your review** with the reason in plain words ("it did not come from the bank's own address", "the amount is very different from the last value"). Choose the account and press **Apply**, or **Ignore** (you can put an ignored message back). **Recorded** lists what was taken in, **Skipped** lists what was set aside and why (not a balance email, older than what you have, ignored by you, reader problems), and **Automation** shows when the email reader, market data, daily summary and rate pages last did their work.

**Forwarding from your own mailboxes.** Bank alerts reach you and Ragini on your own addresses, and Gmail filters forward them (with the label) to the app's mailbox. Because the forward comes from you, the app looks inside it for the original sender and checks that against the bank. Every household login address counts as your own mailbox; if you forward from another address, add it under **Settings > Bank e-mail forwarding**. A forward from any other address is never trusted, and a forward whose original sender is not the bank still waits for review.

**Read again, check the number, correct it.** Under every message there is **Show the email**: a kept copy of the text (account numbers cut to the last four digits, no attachments) with the number that was read highlighted, so you can check it at a glance. **Read again** (on anything that is skipped, waiting or failed) makes the AI read just that one email; if it finds a balance for one account, the number is recorded straight away without waiting for you. If it is wrong, type the right number next to the email and press **Save number**: the corrected figure replaces it and the earlier one stays in the history as replaced. Emails logged before copies were kept are fetched from Gmail again at the next mailbox run (within 3 hours).

**Why a fetched email may not change a balance.** A value counts by its date: the latest date wins. If the email is dated 25 Oct and you imported or typed a value dated 26 Oct, the email is older and does not become the current balance; the account line then says "email 25 Oct is older". A balance the app added today carries the tag "updated today". When you press **Apply**, **Read again** or **Save number**, you are saying that email is the one to use, so it replaces a typed or imported value with a later date (that value stays in the history as replaced, and the message tells you what was replaced). A newer value from the bank itself is never replaced.

## Import

Open **Import**, press **Choose a file** and pick whatever you have: your Excel workbook (.xlsx), a fund statement (PDF), a fund app export (CSV) or a tax statement (Form 26AS or AIS). The page works out which one it is from the file's name and first lines (in your browser; nothing is uploaded yet) and shows the matching form with the file already attached. If it cannot tell, it asks "What is this file?"; you can also correct a wrong guess. Below the box there is a card for each kind if you prefer to choose yourself.

- **Excel workbook**: _Update order, names and links_ makes the screens follow your sheet (order, NRE or NRO, joint, which savings account each deposit belongs to, deposit numbers as last four digits, notes). It never changes a balance and is safe to repeat. _Import balances and deposits_ is for the first time or a new household: it shows a preview with checks against your own sheet and saves nothing until you press Import. The same file cannot be imported twice.
- **Excel workbook, _History from all monthly tabs_**: brings in the older months of the same workbook, so the cash and deposit history and growth are visible. See "History from all monthly tabs" below.
- **Fund statement**: a CAMS/KFintech/MF Central PDF. Choose whose it is (taken from the file name when possible) and type the password you chose when you requested it, usually your PAN in capitals; it is used once and never saved. Press **Preview changes**: the statement is compared with what the app already has and only what is new or changed is listed (see "Preview changes" under Fund statements below). Nothing is saved until you press **Apply the selected changes**. Statements forwarded by email are read by themselves and the changes are applied at once, with a line saying what changed.
- **Fund app export (CSV)** (Kuvera layout: Date, Folio Number, Name of the Fund, Order, Units, NAV, Current Nav, Amount): choose all the files at once, one per person (the first name in the file name is pre-selected). Press **Preview changes** to see, per person and fund, what the files would change compared with what you have (a new fund, new transactions, units and amount before and after, a SIP that stopped or changed); untick anything that is wrong and press **Apply the selected changes**. Folios of the same fund are combined; importing a file twice adds nothing twice; digital gold (no folio) is left out.
- **Tax statement**: Form 26AS or the AIS. The date of birth saved in Settings opens a protected PDF.

Each kind keeps its own page (open its card) with the last result and anything that needs a look.

### History from all monthly tabs

**What to upload.** The same workbook ("Summary - Bank Reports") with one tab per month, as you already keep it. Tabs may be in any order, some months may be missing, and the layout may have changed over the years (a column added, Notes moved, "Type" renamed "Category"); the headings of each tab are read, not fixed column letters. Choose the file on Import, pick **History from all monthly tabs** and press **Read every tab (nothing is saved)**.

**The preview.** One line per month: the tab, the date it stands for and where that date came from, rows read, how many are accounts already on file, how many belong to a closed deposit or account that would be added, how many could not be matched, and a check against the tab's own total (a tick, or a cross with the difference in rupees). When the workbook has a Summary tab, each month's total there is checked too. A month that does not add up is not selected for you. The headline says, for example, "28 months found, Jun 2021 to Oct 2026; every month adds up; 69 closed deposits would be added; 0 conflicts".

**Which date a tab gets.** The Summary tab's "Date of Checking" for that month, else the date beside "Current" or "This Time" on the tab, as long as it falls in the tab's month (a date left over from an earlier copy is ignored and the page says so); otherwise the month end, and never later than today.

**Tabs it will not trust.** A tab whose title cannot be right is flagged and left out until you say which month it is: one that sits out of place among the tabs (a January between March and December of another year) or whose contents are newer than its title (a deposit that starts months after the month it is named for; the allowed lateness is Settings > Money rules > "A monthly tab is flagged when a deposit on it starts more than", 62 days). Choose the month from the list on that line (the suggestion comes first, then "as titled"); the page reads the tabs again and the tab becomes an ordinary month. A **Copy of ...** tab is shown but never selected, and a value it has is never allowed to replace one from the original.

**What it does.**
- Adds a dated balance (or value) for each account, third-party holding and loan that matches one on file: the date of the tab, source "workbook", marked unverified, remembered as `hist:<tab>:<cell>`. Importing the same file again adds nothing: a value already on file for the same month with the same amount is left alone.
- Adds, as closed records, deposits and savings accounts that older tabs list and later ones no longer do (and that are not on file). The tabs never say when a deposit was paid out, so its end date is the first month the tabs stop listing it (its maturity date when that falls in between): an estimate, flagged on the record, which you can edit. A deposit whose tabs give no start date is never given an invented one: it takes the start from another tab, else the first month it is listed, and says so. A deposit that disappears in the month another of the same bank and person appears is shown as a possible renewal; nothing is linked for you.
- Keeps each mutual fund's monthly figure in the sheet only to check against the statement ("the sheet said ₹X on that date; the statement-based value is ₹Y"). It never replaces the statement or price-based value, and a fund is never created from a monthly line.
- For a deposit already on file it only fills in a missing deposit number; its terms are never changed.

**What it never does.** It never changes or deletes a value. It never touches a value on a later date. It never replaces a value you or a bank email entered unless you choose the sheet's value for that item. It never guesses between two accounts: a line that two accounts could be is identified from the other tabs where they allow it (see "Which account is each savings line?"), and otherwise is listed under "Lines that could not be matched" with its cell and written to neither. It never stores the workbook.

**Which account is each savings line?** Older tabs write a plain "Cash" under a bank where you have both an NRE and an NRO account (or several). The newer tabs name the accounts (last four digits, NRE/NRO, JO), and the accounts are the same ones, so the page works out which account an older line is from the tabs on either side of it. Each such line is shown under **Which account is each savings line?** with a small chip, the reason in words and the tabs it came from:
- **inferred** (ticked): the tab has as many lines at that bank and person as the nearest tab before and the nearest tab after, in the same order, so each line is the account at its position ("Same position (1st of 2) in Sample Bank - Anil as in the June 2021 and Dec 2021 tabs, where it is the NRE account Cash-NRE-1100"). It is saved like any other value. Untick it to leave the line out.
- **inferred, likely** (not ticked): the number of lines differs because an account was opened or closed in between (the order of the accounts is kept and the account open on both sides must have a line), or order left two accounts and the balance next door is clearly closer to one of them, or only one side names the accounts. Tick it to use it.
- **which account?**: more than one account fits and the tabs do not settle it. Choose from the list ("Fits this line", then the bank's other accounts), or leave it. Tick **Also for every tab where "Cash" sits in the same place** to give the same answer to the same line in the other tabs.
- **not inferred**: something contradicts it: the line says NRE but its place in the other tabs is the NRO account, the tabs on either side disagree about the accounts, or the balance is far from what that account held next door (Settings > Money rules, "Estimated balances"). The reason is shown and you can still choose.
Your choices are part of the plan, so reading the tabs again or importing again gives the same answers, and a value already saved for a cell keeps its account. A value saved for an inferred or chosen line carries a note that the account was not stated on the sheet and how it was found. It follows the same rules as every other value: a different value already on file for that day is a conflict, and nothing is overwritten. Lines that match a single account only because they do not say "joint" are still matched as before; if the other tabs point to a different account, that month's Notes say "Check it".

**Conflicts.** If a different value is already on file for the same account and the same day, it is listed with both numbers and the source of the one on file. Choose **Keep what is on file** (the default) or **Use the sheet**; the second adds the sheet's value and marks the old one as replaced (the old one stays in the history).

**Importing.** Select the months (ticked for you: months that add up and are not in doubt) and press **Import the selected months**. They are imported one at a time, oldest first, with a progress bar, because a hosted request has a time limit. A month that fails does not lose the others; press the button again and the finished months add nothing while the rest continue. The file is sent again for each month and is never kept.

**Reading the coverage grid.** After an import, **History coverage** (on Import, and linked from Accounts, Deposits and Cash) shows every savings or cash account and every deposit as a row of small cells, one per month: green = a balance is recorded that month, purple = a deposit is held (its value follows from its terms), red hatching = a missing month between the first and last balance, amber = an open account not updated since, grey = before it started or after it closed. Rows with the most holes come first and each row says in words where it begins and which months are missing. Months you have no tab for show up as gaps; they are filled by a later import or by the Monthly update.

**Growth figures and the net worth line.** Everything that looks back (the profit by period on Home, "what changed", the last 30 days and the financial year) is worked out from the balances, deposit terms and fund units on file for the earlier day, so importing history makes earlier periods work at once: nothing waits for a daily snapshot. A closed deposit added from history counts its interest until its end date.

**Rebuild the history chart.** The net worth line on Home is drawn from saved points. After the last month is imported the app works out net worth for every month end the records reach back to and saves each as a point marked **rebuilt from history** (the note under the chart counts them). The button **Rebuild the history chart** on the History coverage panel does the same whenever you like: it only adds points, a point saved on the day (a daily save, a monthly update, an import) is never replaced, and pressing it again changes nothing. A fund whose value in the sheet is the only record for an old month is valued from that sheet figure and marked est.

## Sync with Google Sheet

Your monthly Google Sheet has one tab per month. **Sync with Google Sheet** (on **Import**, and as a button in the header of **Monthly update**) writes the app's figures into this month's tab, in your sheet's own format, so you no longer type them twice.

1. **Preview sync** shows, row by row, what would change (old → new), new rows, rows closed in the app, rows it could not match and accounts to place by hand. Nothing is written.
2. **Confirm and write to Google Sheet** does it. If this month's tab does not exist, the latest earlier month is copied first (so the layout and formulas stay); if it exists, a hidden copy named `Oct 2026 - before sync` is kept and the data is replaced. Syncing twice gives the same sheet. Other months are never touched, rows are never deleted, and cells with formulas are never overwritten.

What goes in: savings and cash balances, S$ balances (the amount and rate in the note), deposits (rate, dates, principal, maturity value, deposit number), funds (value, invested, start date, the "(STOPPED)" mark), money lent and money held by others. Account numbers and PANs are never written. To undo a sync, unhide the `… - before sync` tab and copy it back (see the runbook).

First time: **Settings > Connections > Google Sheet** has the steps (create a free Google service account, enable the Sheets API, share the sheet with its e-mail as Editor, paste the sheet's link and the key) and a **Test connection** button. The full guide is `docs/runbooks/google-sheet-sync.md`.

## Cash, and the total on Accounts

**Cash** (the *Savings and cash* tile on Accounts opens it) is to savings what Deposits is to fixed deposits: the cash available today, what the balances are estimated to earn (a year, a month, a day), which bank holds how much with each bank's share and average rate, the balances that are out of date, the largest balance, the split by person and by NRE and NRO, and a register of every savings account with its rate, last recorded balance, value today and interest a year. The person filter narrows all of it. Interest is an estimate: balance times rate, less the tax at source set in Settings.

The total on Cash stays all the cash in rupees. A foreign-currency account is counted in it at today's rate and its balance is listed on its own under the total (`S$ 41,250`, then `≈ ₹23.14 L at 1 SGD = ₹56.1, rate of 26 Oct`); in the register of accounts a foreign account shows its balance in its own currency, and under the rupee value the rate it is worth at.

At the bottom of **Accounts** a **Total** shows invested or balance, what everything pays at maturity, and today's value, for the lines counted. It follows the person you choose and the search or column filters: type a bank name and the total becomes that bank's. It uses the same rule as the overview at the top, so the two always agree.

## Fixed deposits

**Deposits** has three tabs: Deposits (every deposit with its bank, owner, rate, maturity date and the amount at maturity), Maturities (what to confirm, what matures soon, a calendar) and Insurance cover. A deposit whose date has passed is not counted anywhere (see "What the numbers mean"); press **Confirm matured** on its line (or on the Maturities tab) once the money has been paid out. A confirmed deposit, or one that matured more than the number of days set in Settings (see below), is hidden from the list; a line under the list says how many and **Show matured** lists them again, dimmed (`?matured=all`). The insured-limit view shows, per bank, each ownership name as written against the ₹5 lakh deposit insurance limit: Rupash, Ragini, Rupash & Ragini and Ragini & Rupash are four different names, each insured separately (so up to ₹20 lakh at one bank), and the order of the names matters. Set the second name of each joint account by pressing **Edit** on it, then **More details** (Joint account, Second name; the swap button reverses the order); until then a joint account is grouped under its first name.

**The overview at the top of Deposits** shows what the fixed deposits are worth today (current value), what you put in (invested) and what they pay at maturity, the profit so far in rupees and per cent, and an estimate of what they earned today (the difference between each deposit's value today and yesterday on its own rate). Below it: **which bank holds how much** (a ranked list with each bank's share, deposits, average rate and next maturity; the link beside it groups the list below by bank), **what matures year by year**, and the smaller figures: interest still to earn, next maturity, deposits maturing within the reminder window, deposits to confirm, the share held at the largest bank and the amount above the insured limit. The split by person and by NRE/NRO sits at the bottom. The person filter narrows the whole overview; the insured limit is always for the whole household.

## Mutual funds and goals

- **Investments** has seven tabs. **Overview** is the first screen: what the funds are worth, the profit or loss in rupees and per cent, today's move, the annual return (XIRR), what selling has booked, the SIP and the long-term share, with goal by goal, movers, the next monthly investments and the people side by side. **Performance** is how they have done over time (below). **Funds** lists every fund on one compact line (figures right-aligned, the units, start and kind in a slim second line) grouped by person, goal, kind of fund or fund house, with totals. **Transactions** is every purchase, instalment, switch, dividend and sale of every fund. **Breakdown** is the statistics page. **Update funds** is the monthly entry of units and prices. **Match funds** matches each fund in your sheet to the official scheme list once; you confirm the match. The old Growth address opens Performance. The person chips narrow every reading tab to one person; each person keeps one colour on every graph.
- **The data check on Investments.** Every figure on the page is worked out from the transactions you gave the app (money in, money out, the fund, the price on the day, the units), so the app first checks whether those transactions are complete. Under each person on the Overview and Funds tabs, a *Data check* line says how many of their funds reconcile with their statements ("11 of 14 funds reconcile with their statements. 3 need earlier history."), shows the earliest transaction on file, and lists the funds that need something and why. Each fund also carries a chip: **✓ Reconciled** (every line follows the one before it and the lines lead to the units the fund holds), **⚠ Starts midway** (some units were already held before the first line on file, so their purchases are missing), **⚠ Gap** (lines are missing, or one is on file twice, between the dates shown), **✗ Doesn't match** (the lines add up but lead to a different number of units than the fund records), **~ Estimated** (every line is assumed from the monthly investment plan) or **– No transactions**. A fund that does not reconcile shows **est.** beside its value, invested amount, profit and annual return, and so do the totals it is part of, because those figures are partly estimated. Nothing is hidden: a mismatch is stated, with both numbers.
  - **How to fix a gap.** Ask CAMS or KFintech for a **detailed** (not summary) statement from the first investment (or from the date set in Settings → Money rules → *Fund history check*, 1 Jan 2021 by default) to today, for each person, and forward it to the inbox label, or import it on the Import page. Importing the same lines again is safe: a line already on file is not added twice. A *Doesn't match* with complete history usually means the unit count on the fund is old or was typed wrongly: edit the fund and enter the units from your latest statement. If a fund has two folios, the lines of each folio are checked separately; the unit count on file may hold only one of them.
  - **A person flagged "earliest transaction is later than 1 Jan 2021".** The earliest transaction on file for them is later than the date in Settings while some of their funds started earlier, so older history is missing even if each fund looks complete.
  - **On a fund's page**, *Does this add up?* shows the first and last transaction, how many lines and from where, the units by the lines, on the last statement line and as recorded, the value of each at the latest price, the cost of the units held by the lines against the invested amount recorded, and the dates where lines do not follow each other. It opens by itself whenever something does not add up.
- **Exit load and tax, on a fund's page.** The Overview tab of a fund starts with *Exit load and tax*. A status line says whether any unit is still under an exit load ("Exit load applies today to 60 units (1%) until 1 Sep 2027"), or that none is, or that the exit load is not set for this fund (the usual rule from Settings is then assumed, and a link lets you set the fund's own). Tiles count the units under the load and free of it, short-term (STCG) and long-term (LTCG) units with their gain, and units at a loss. The table lists each purchase still held: units, cost, value today, gain, days held, the date it is free of exit load, the date it turns long-term, and chips for its state today. For equity funds, *Tax-gain harvest this year* uses the same plan as the Tax page: how many units and rupees to sell now to use the person's tax-free long-term allowance (oldest, load-free, long-term units first; never units still under an exit load), what it costs, the next dates more units become ready, or "Nothing to do now". Debt funds are shown at the slab rate and international or gold funds use their own holding period from Settings; neither has the yearly allowance. *How much to take out?* takes an amount and lists the units to sell, the exit load and an approximate tax. If the purchases are estimated, or the history does not add up, the panel says so and carries the **est.** mark. The app never sells anything.
- **Funds without a price (Investments, Match funds tab).** A fund's value is its units times the day's price, and the price comes from the official list of schemes (AMFI) once the fund is matched to its scheme. A fund that is not matched has **no price source**: it keeps the value you last recorded, labelled that way, and its history and gains are less reliable. The tab lists each such fund with its folio (last four digits), units or amount invested and the last recorded value, and for each one says **what I found**, **what I suggest and why**, and gives an **Approve** button that names the scheme and its AMFI code ("Approve: use Axis Large Cap Fund - Direct Plan - Growth Option (AMFI code 120465)"). How sure the suggestion is depends on the evidence: **certain** when the fund's ISIN (saved on the fund, read from a fund statement you imported, or on the statement you are previewing) is the scheme's ISIN in the official list; **very likely** when the fund's name is the scheme's name including Direct or Regular and Growth or IDCW; **likely** when the words and the fund house fit and the plan comes from the name or from the plan saved on the fund (Growth is taken when the name does not say IDCW, and the reason says so). A name never decides between Direct and Regular, or between Growth and IDCW: when the evidence does not point to one scheme the portal says "I cannot tell: please choose", shows the best three with how they differ ("Regular instead of Direct", "IDCW instead of Growth") and has no Approve button. Renamed schemes are found ("Axis Bluechip" is now "Axis Large Cap"; Mid Cap, Midcap and Mid-Cap are the same). Under the choices there is a **search box** (by name, ISIN or AMFI code, best 10) for a scheme that is not listed, and **This is not a listed fund** for a fund that has no official scheme (it keeps its last recorded value, labelled "no price source", and is no longer counted as waiting; **Look for a scheme again** undoes it). **Approve all certain matches** applies every ISIN-certain suggestion at once. Approving sets the fund's scheme and its ISIN (an ISIN already read from a statement is never replaced), then fetches that scheme's price history once so the value follows the market at once (if that fails, tonight's update does it); pressing Approve again changes nothing and nothing is deleted. A fund statement (CAS) you import does the same by itself: funds matched by ISIN get their scheme and ISIN, and any it cannot place appear on this tab with the statement's ISIN as the certain suggestion. The number of funds still waiting is on Home under **Needs you** ("3 funds have no price source: match them") and in the daily e-mail.
- **Goals** has one progress card per goal: how much of the cost is set aside today, how much the projection covers, the extra to put in each month, and the funds set aside for it. Add a goal with just a name; set a target and date later under *Edit this goal*. The second tab, *Money not set aside*, lists each holding with the share no goal claims, so you can set it aside in one step. A holding can be split across goals but never above 100%.


### Value by period (Investments)

The **Value by period** tab shows what the mutual funds were worth at the end of every week, month, quarter or year, as a bar chart (**Total valuation**) over a table: **Period**, **Portfolio value (₹)**, **Change (₹)**, **Change (%)**, **Money put in, net of sales (₹)**, **Gain (₹)** and **Gain (%)**. The first two change columns are the plain move in value, as a fund statement shows it, so they include new money; the money column and the last two take it out, so a big purchase never reads as a good month. Gain = change − money put in + money taken out, as a share of the money at work in the period (the same function as the profit on Home and the Performance tab).

- **Weeks** are Monday to Sunday, labelled by the Monday ("Week of 5 Oct 2026"); **months** are calendar months; **quarters** follow the financial year (April to June is Q1) unless Settings > Fund history check says calendar quarters; **years** are financial years from 1 April.
- The range is the last 12, the last 24 or all (the 12 is a setting), or two dates you choose. The person filter is the same as on the other tabs. Each choice is a link or a plain form, so it has an address and works without scripts. **Download CSV** gives the same table for the same choices.
- The latest row stops at today and says "(to date)"; the first row starts where the records do ("data starts ..."). A period with no price or record near its end says "no data" instead of zero. A value that rests on a record moved forward with the price, a plan or assumed instalments is marked est.
- Negative numbers are red with a minus; amounts use Indian grouping. On a phone each period is a small card.

## Money at work

**Money at work** (menu, next to *Can I retire now?*) has two tabs.

**Is my money growing?** lists every counted holding, slowest first, with the yearly rate it earns **now** and where that rate comes from: a deposit's own rate; a savings account's own rate, else the rates list for its bank, kind and balance; a fund's own yearly return (XIRR), else the long-run fund return of your retirement plan; the growth assumed in Settings for property, NPS/ULIP and other holdings; nothing for money lent, money held by someone else and foreign cash with no rate. Each rate is shown before tax, after tax (NRO interest loses the tax deducted at source; NRE interest is tax-free; fund gains are taxed only when sold) and after inflation (the retirement plan's). Holdings are grouped: **not growing** (no return), **slow** (below inflation after tax), **growing**, and **growing well** (beating inflation by at least the margin in Settings → Estimated balances). The strip at the top shows the pace of all your money after tax, the money in each group, what slow money loses to rising prices each year, and what the suggested moves would add. **What would make it grow** gives each move in words: savings above the buffer (Settings → rates) placed in a deposit at a bank that pays clearly more, a savings balance that would earn more at another bank, a deposit to move when it matures, always inside the insurance limit and only when the gain is worth it (Settings → rates); and a line for each fund returning less than inflation and each holding that earns nothing. Nothing is moved for you.

**Corpus in 20 years** projects today's money, year by year, with interest and gains staying invested: each holding grows at its own rate after tax (deposits renew at the same rate), the SIPs running today continue and rise by the plan's yearly step-up. One graph and one table show these lines: **the same pace, funds at their actual past return** (every holding at its own rate; the funds' own past return, weighted by value, is in the heading), **savings and deposits too at the funds' actual past return** (your savings, deposits and funds all grown at that same past return, as if the cash were invested like the funds and taxed only when sold; property, retirement accounts and money lent keep their own pace), **funds at the long-run return** (every fund at your retirement plan's long-run return instead of its own past one), **2% and 1% slower**, **1% and 2% faster** (every growing holding moved by those points; money that earns nothing stays at nothing), and **if the slow money is moved** (the moves on the first tab made today). Ages come from Settings → People; the last column is the same pace in today's rupees. A second table splits the same pace into money added, growth, deposits, funds, cash and others. Funds' own past returns can be far above what the next 20 years bring, so the long-run fund return of your plan is shown beside them. The number of years is in Settings → Estimated balances. **Download Excel** gives both tables; **Print or save as PDF** prints the page.

## Daily summary

**Settings > Notifications** turns the daily email on, adds up to five extra recipients and chooses what it shows: **Include the goals view** and **Include the retirement view** (both on unless you switch them off). Who receives it is changed there too.

The email is built to be read in a minute on a phone. In order, it shows:

1. **The header**: the date, "values as of" the newest recorded value, and a chip saying how many inputs are late.
2. **Net worth**, large, with **Today** (what the banks and deposits earned plus how the funds moved, in rupees and per cent) and the **overall profit or loss** on the money you put in. These are the same figures as the top of Home.
3. **Four tiles**: in FDs, in savings, in mutual funds, in other. Each shows its amount, its share of the total and today's change; FDs and funds also show their overall profit and per cent. The four add up to net worth.
4. **Goals**: one card per goal with the target and date, how much is saved so far, a progress bar and a chip: **On track**, **Ahead by** an amount, **Behind by** an amount (with about how many months late), or **Not enough data** (set a target amount and date). A sentence says what the current pace reaches by the date against what is needed, and for a goal that is behind, how much more to add each month. It uses the same arithmetic and assumptions as the Goals page.
5. **Retirement**, as a goal: the corpus needed against what your savings and SIPs reach by the retirement age (both in today's money), whether that is on track, ahead or short, years to go, the chance the money lasts (the same 2,000-path run as "Can I retire now?") and about how much more to save each month to close a gap. It appears only when you have saved retirement assumptions.
6. **Needs you** (the top five, ranked as on Home, each a link to the page where it is dealt with) and **What changed** (the biggest moves since a month ago, and the largest deposits paying out in the next 30 days).
7. **The footer**: an Open the portal button, where the numbers come from (estimated or recorded, and the date of each market input), and where to change settings.

It says plainly when no new bank balance has arrived, never presents an estimate as a bank balance and never prints a full account number. Matured deposits waiting to be confirmed are not counted, the same as on the portal. A phone in dark mode shows it in dark colours.

The subject reads `Family Wealth Summary · 27 Oct 2026 · Net worth ₹4.82 Cr (+0.4% today) · 2 need you`. The day's percentage goes first when the line is too long for a phone, then the "need you" note; the date always stays. A Gmail filter or label can match "Family Wealth Summary".

**Preview today's e-mail** (Settings > Notifications, or `/settings/notifications/preview`) shows today's message built now from your own figures, on a phone and on a laptop, without sending anything. A sample with invented figures is at `/demo/digest`. To get a real test in your own inbox, see `docs/runbooks/daily-summary.md` ("digest test"). **Share** gives a short version for WhatsApp, with the same numbers as the Telegram message.

## Tax harvesting and refunds

**Harvest plan (top of Tax → What to sell).** One table for the whole family: a row per fund per person (**Whose**), the gains to book inside each person's yearly tax-free allowance and the losses worth booking. Each row gives the **units to sell** (three decimals, as the sell screen wants them), the price used, the **cost** of those units, their **value now**, the **long-term** and **short-term** gain (a loss shows as a minus), the **exit load**, the day to **sell by** (the order date, kept clear of 31 March by Settings → "Place orders this many days before 31 March"), what to **buy back** and roughly how many units that buys at today's price, the day to **buy back** (Settings → "Buy the fund back this many working days after selling", 2 by default, so the money is in your bank first), the day the units bought back are **long-term again**, and the **tax saved** (on a gain: the long-term tax it avoids later; on a loss: the tax it saves when set off against gains). Under the table each person's line sums the gain booked, the loss booked, the money to put back and the allowance left. **Download Excel** gives the same table as a workbook; **Print or save as PDF** prints it. The cards below keep the reasons, checks and lot-by-lot working.

**Selling and buying back does not change your return.** A fund's yearly return (XIRR) counts every purchase and sale on its date, so a sale and a buy-back a few days later cancel out; the cost of the units goes up (that is the point), the profit on the amount invested now goes down by the gain booked, and **Profit booked by selling** goes up by the same amount. On Home, a purchase of the same fund within Settings → "A buy-back within this many days of a sale is a reinvestment" (10 days by default) of a sale is the sale money going back in: your own money and the yearly return are not changed by it, even when the sale is on 31 March and the buy-back on 2 April. When one of the person's savings balances was recorded between the sale and the buy-back, that balance may already hold the money, so nothing more is added. As non-residents, the fund house deducts tax at source on the gain even inside the allowance: that money comes back with the refund (Refunds tab), so until then a little less goes back into the fund.

**Tax** (tab *What to sell*) answers one question per person: which units to sell this year, and how many. Each person's block opens with
one sentence (for example "Asha can book ₹81,000 of tax-free gain this year"), a bar of the yearly free allowance (used / this plan /
still free) and a **Sell this** list: fund, the exact **units to sell** (plain digits with a Copy button, to type into the platform's sell screen),
what they are worth, the gain locked in tax-free, and plain checks: held over the long-term period (Settings, Tax rules), no exit load (or the load that
applies), within the free allowance, and whether the purchases come from your fund statement or are estimated from your SIP history.
**Show the working** under each fund lists the purchases the units come from, oldest first, with date, cost and gain per purchase, the long- and
short-term split, the costs, and the amount to put back afterwards. **Do not sell yet** lists funds with short-term units or an exit load still
running, with the date each turns ready. **Losses you can book** says in plain words which units would book a loss and whether it saves tax this
year. **On the platform** is a five-line checklist. A small footnote at the bottom gives the costs to expect (stamp duty, transaction tax, exit load).
Non-resident tax withheld is in the person's working and each harvest creates a
**Tax refunds to claim** record by itself (expected tax withheld and expected refund; tab *Refunds*). Update it as the sale happens, the return is filed
and the refund arrives; **Download as CSV** gives it to your adviser. NRO interest tax is handled on the filing papers because the refund
depends on your total Indian income. The app prepares figures; the return itself is filed on the income-tax portal using Form 26AS and the
Annual Information Statement, which the app cannot download for you.

## Fund statements (CAS) and exact harvesting

Ask CAMS/KFintech (or MF Central) for a **detailed** statement from the first investment to today, for each person, and forward the
e-mail to your `FWOS/Inbox` label. The inbox job opens it with the PAN saved in Settings, finds each fund by ISIN (or name and folio),
adds funds it does not know, stores the transactions it does not have yet and updates what is new or changed (units, invested amount, first purchase date, SIP and value). You can also upload
the PDF under **Import** (type the PAN once to open it; it is not saved) and look at the changes before they are saved. From then on harvesting works from real purchase lots, oldest units
first, includes funds with a running SIP, and counts long-term gains you have already realised this year.

### SIP changes, estimated instalments and funds missing from a statement

- **Preview changes (Import > Fund statement, and Fund app export).** The statement or file is read against what is already on file; a detailed statement wins over lines that came from somewhere less exact (see "Which record wins" below), and an app export never replaces anything. A statement can hold the folios of several people (the ones registered under one email address): each folio goes to the person whose PAN is printed on it, there is one card per person, and folios of someone who is not in your family list are named and skipped. For each person the preview lists only the funds with something new or changed, as one row each with chips: **New fund**, **Sold**, **SIP stopped**, **SIP new**, **SIP resumed**, **SIP ₹5,000 → ₹8,000**, **+5 transactions**. A row shows units and the amount invested before and after, the value on the statement date, and, under "new transactions", the lines that will be added. Funds with nothing new are folded under "N funds unchanged, left alone" and are not touched. Every changed fund has a tick and so does every SIP verdict, all ticked at first; untick what is wrong and press **Apply the selected changes**. Only the ticked items are saved, and only the values that really changed are written. A line already on file is never added twice (a line with the same date, units and amount counts as the same line even if it came from another file or app), and a line on file from an earlier statement that this statement does not have is kept: the row says "3 lines on file are not on this statement; kept". Lines from an app export, the workbook, an estimate or typed by hand that a detailed statement does not have are replaced, as described below. Overruling a verdict ("this SIP is not stopped", "this fund is not sold") by unticking it is remembered, so the same statement does not propose it again; a later statement with new facts can. A price that moved is not a change in itself (units times the latest price already follows the market), so a fund whose lines, units and amount invested are as they were is left alone. A unit count you typed with a later date than the statement is not replaced by it, and a file that starts long after the fund did cannot lower the units or the amount invested. Emailed statements are applied by themselves (nothing to tick), and the Recorded tab of the Inbox and Import > Fund statement > Recent statements say what changed, for example "2 funds updated: +5 transactions; 1 SIP stopped; 1 fund sold", with the full list behind "What changed".
- **When the app cannot be sure, it suggests and asks you.** A row with a **Your decision** chip (or "Not on this statement") never just says "check this". It says **What I found**, **What I suggest** and why, an **Approve: …** button and your other choices, and nothing is changed until you decide:
  - *A statement folio that could be more than one of your funds* (two funds with the same ISIN, or a fund added twice, and the folio on file does not tell them apart). The row lists your candidates told apart by folio, units and start date. I suggest the one the evidence points to, strongest first: a fund with that ISIN holds exactly the units the statement closes with ("Folio …3820 is not on file yet, but your fund … has 3,047.333 units, which equal the statement's closing units"); two folios of one scheme together hold the units of one fund (they are one holding: combine them); or only one fund has that name. (When one of the funds already has that folio on file, it is matched without asking.) With none of these it says "I cannot tell which it is, so I am not guessing: please choose." and offers no Approve button. Your choices: **Same as** each candidate, **A new fund**, or **Leave it alone for now**. Pressing Approve (or picking another answer) shows the fund as it would be after Apply, with its changes; Apply then compares and saves it like any matched fund and remembers that this folio belongs to that fund, so the same question is not asked on the next statement. Nothing is deleted, and applying the same answer again changes nothing.
  - *A fund on file that the statement does not list.* On a statement for everything you hold (keyed on your PAN), I suggest marking it sold on the statement's date (ticked, as before). On a statement keyed on an e-mail address, one with a scheme it could not read cleanly, or one that misses most of your funds, I suggest leaving it as it is and say why. You can choose **Mark as sold** and type the date, or **Leave as it is**.
  - *A SIP the statement could not judge* ("Cannot tell yet"). I suggest keeping the SIP as recorded; you can choose **Mark the SIP as stopped** and type the date (it starts with the first instalment that did not come).
  - *Folios of someone who is not in your family list* (on a statement keyed on an e-mail address). They are never applied; I suggest skipping them, with an **Add this person in Settings > People** link if they should be in the household.
  A statement that arrives by e-mail is applied without a person present, so questions are left open and listed as "waiting for your decision"; upload that statement under Import to see the suggestion and answer. In the same way, **Bank emails** (Needs your review) suggests the account and amount for each waiting balance with an **Approve: record …** button, and **History from all monthly tabs** starts each "value that disagrees with what is on file" on the answer I suggest, with the reason.
- **How a SIP is called stopped, new, resumed or changed.** Never from a fixed number of days: each SIP is judged by its own instalments (the lines marked as SIP on the statement and those already on file). Its usual gap is the middle gap between its instalments (about a month, a week or a quarter, whatever it really is), and its usual lateness is the most an instalment has strayed from its due date in its own history (weekends and holidays move a debit by a few days). **SIP stopped**: the next due date after the last instalment has passed, plus that SIP's usual lateness, with no instalment, on a statement that covers that date. The stop is recorded from the first missed due date. If the due date has passed but the usual lateness has not, or the statement ends before the due date, the app says "Cannot tell yet" and changes nothing; a statement that starts after the missed date cannot show it either. A fund that is fully sold is stopped and sold together, and a statement that states in words that the SIP was cancelled is an explicit stop. **SIP new**: no SIP on file and at least 3 similar instalments in a row at one gap (Settings → Estimated balances → "A SIP is recognised after this many instalments in a row"); one-off purchases are not SIPs. **SIP resumed**: a SIP marked stopped, and a run of instalments again after the stop. **SIP amount changed**: the latest instalments settle on another amount than the one in force on file on that date (the dated history decides, so an old statement does not undo a recorded change); it is dated from the first instalment at the new amount. A fund with no SIP instalments on the statement or on file is not judged at all: its SIP stays as you recorded it, and the fund page says "expected; not verified by a statement".
- **When a fund is called sold.** In two ways: the statement lists the fund with zero units after a sale (a folio that closes at zero is a real sale, and is ticked), or the fund is missing from a **complete** fund statement. A statement only proves a fund is gone when it lists everything the person holds, which is the case only when it says it was made **from the PAN**. A statement made **from an email address** (the usual CAMS plus KFintech one) lists only the folios registered under that email, so a fund that is not on it may simply be registered under another email: it is shown as **Not on this statement: check** (not ticked, never called sold), with the reason. The same is done, even for a PAN statement, when more than half of the person's active funds are missing from it (that is more likely a partial statement than that many sales), or when a scheme on it could not be read cleanly (a name that is statement text, no closing balance), or when a fund held on the statement could not be matched to one of yours (it might be the missing fund under a new name), or when the statement lists only balances held with a depository, and never for a fund started or counted after the statement ended. When a fund really is sold, the row says why ("Not on the statement dated 31 Oct 2026, which lists all your funds.") and you can untick it.
- **Changing a SIP.** When you raise, lower, stop or restart a SIP (Investments, update a fund), the change is written down with the date it takes effect (today unless you set a date). The instalments already made keep the amount that was in force on their day, so a new amount never rewrites the past. A stopped SIP adds no instalments after its stop date.
- **Changing a fund (the fund's own page).**
  - **SIP plan tab.** Lists every dated change of the SIP: earlier ones, the one in force now, and planned ones. **Change the amount** or **Stop it** from any date (in the past, or up to two years ahead). **Pause it** from a date with the date it **starts again**: nothing is assumed in between, and on the restart date it runs again by itself at the amount it had before the pause (the daily job brings the fund's "SIP a month" up to date that morning). **Take back** removes a change entered by mistake; the amount the SIP started at stays while later changes depend on it. Past instalments always keep the amount of their day.
  - **Paid from.** Choose the savings account the SIP is debited from. Each instalment then comes off that account's estimated balance on its date, after its last recorded balance, so the money is not counted both in the bank and in the fund until you next update the balance. An instalment already on a bank statement (a recorded movement from that account into the fund, within 5 days and the same amount) is not taken off twice.
  - **Correct or delete a transaction.** Open a line (Transactions tab → the line). A line typed in, or from an app export, can be **corrected** (date, type, amount, units, price) or **deleted**; the fund's units and amount invested move with it exactly. A line from a fund statement can only be **deleted**: the next statement that covers its date brings it back as the statement has it, so the statement always wins. An estimated instalment is changed through the SIP plan. Every addition, correction and deletion is in the fund's activity log, with the figures before and after.
  - **Sold every unit.** When adding a sale or switch out, tick **Sold every unit**: no units are asked for (they are the units held on the date, from the real lines, or the recorded count when the fund has no lines), the gain is booked from the lots, the fund is closed and its SIP stopped from that day.
- **Assumed instalments are saved as estimates.** For a fund with counted units and a running SIP, the instalments assumed since the units were last counted (each at the price of its day) are kept in the transaction list, worded "Estimated SIP instalment, not yet on a statement", so lots, returns and graphs agree with the value shown. They are never shown as paid on the SIP calendar. As soon as a statement or an entry covers a date, the estimates up to it are removed and the real lines take their place; the daily job keeps the rest up to date.
- **Funds treated as sold are closed.** A fund called sold has its status closed, its units and value set to zero on the statement date and its SIP stopped from that date; the estimated instalments after it are removed. The emailed import lists each one ("assumed sold") so you can correct a mistake.

### Which record wins

A **detailed** fund statement (CAS) is the most accurate record there is: it comes from the registrars themselves. So for every fund on one, the statement decides, and anything less exact that disagrees with it is replaced. When two sources disagree, the higher one in this table wins:

| Rank | Source | Stored as | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fund statement (CAS) | `cas` | a detailed statement from CAMS, KFintech or MF Central |
| 2 | App export | `csv` | a transactions file from an investment app (Kuvera and others) |
| 3 | Excel workbook | `workbook` | the family workbook |
| 4 | Estimated | `estimate` | worked out from the start date and the monthly investment |
| 5 | Typed by hand | `manual` | entered by hand |

For each fund that is on a detailed statement (one with transaction lines) the statement decides: the **units held** (its closing units), the **amount invested**, the **first purchase** (the start date), the **value** (units times the price on its date), the **SIP** (amounts, dates, cancellations) and the **lines inside the period it covers**. A line on file inside that period that came from the app export, the workbook, an estimate or was typed by hand, and that the statement does not have (a line with the same date and units, within the tolerance in Settings, counts as the statement's own and is **confirmed**), is **replaced**: it is set aside, not deleted. On the fund page, Transactions tab, a collapsed group "Replaced by the statement" lists each such line with its date, where it came from and the statement that replaced it, so nothing is lost; it is left out of every figure (returns, lots, tax harvesting, reports).

What is *not* replaced:

- Lines **before** the statement's period (a statement that begins mid-way, with units already held, does not touch the earlier history; the earlier units are carried as "opening" as before). A statement printed "from the beginning" (CAMS prints 1 Oct 1986) is the whole history.
- Lines **after** the statement's period.
- Lines from an **earlier statement** that a newer one does not repeat: they stay, with a note.
- **Everything, when the statement does not add up.** If its own lines do not lead from its opening units to its closing units (within the unit tolerance in Settings), nothing is replaced, the period is not recorded, and the row says so in a warning.
- Anything from a statement **dated before** the one already applied. The newer statement's units, amount invested, first purchase and SIP stay, and lines of the older statement that fall inside the period the newer one covers are not added. A statement dated the same day or later wins.
- An **app export** never replaces a line, and it keeps what a statement dated on or after the export's last line decided: units, amount invested, first purchase and the lines inside the statement's period are left as they are. The preview says so ("Kept: the fund statement dated 27 Oct 2026 is more accurate than this file"). A file with lines newer than the statement adds those lines and may update the units.
- The Excel workbook import only creates items; it never overwrites a fund that a statement has decided.

In the preview each fund that has lines to replace shows "This statement replaces 3 lines on file (from 2 app export, 1 estimated), adds 5, confirms 41", with the first lines listed. It has its own tick, ticked at first because the statement is the top priority; untick it to keep your own lines for that fund (the statement's lines are still added). At the top of the preview: "The statement is the most accurate record. Where it differs from what you have, the statement wins; your older lines are kept in a hidden list, not deleted." Importing the same statement twice changes nothing.

## Returns, capital gains and the summary

- **Investments** shows the annual return (XIRR) per fund, per person and for the household. Where a fund has a statement (or imported transactions) the figure comes from the real transactions; for the others it is estimated from the start date and SIP and is marked **estimated** (a `~`, or an orange *estimated* tag). Nothing is drawn from data the app does not have: a graph that needs a statement says so instead.
- **Two kinds of return.** *Time-weighted* shows how the funds themselves did, whatever the dates you put money in (the period tables and the month grid). *Money-weighted (XIRR)* shows what your own money earned, given when each rupee went in (the headline). A fund's published price return ignores your timing too.
- **Performance** (Investments): *Return over time* for 1 week, 1, 3 and 6 months, this year, this financial year, 1, 3 and 5 years and since the start (under a year the plain change, from a year the rate per year), as a table and bars, with the profit in rupees and the **benchmark** beside it. The benchmark is an index fund that follows the Nifty 50, a stand-in for the index (it carries the fund's small costs); until the daily job has fetched its prices the columns are left out. *Profit and loss by period*: switch between week, month, quarter, financial year (April to March) and calendar year; green and red bars with the running total as a line, and a table with rupees, per cent, money added and value at the end. Money you put in or take out is never counted as profit. *Compare* this period with the one before, one person with another, or fund with fund. Periods that rest on funds with no statement are marked **est.** *Month by month*: a grid of each month's return with the year's total (financial or calendar years), the best and worst month and year. *Growth of 100 against the benchmark*, *fall from the highest point* (drawdown), *risk* (volatility, Sharpe and Sortino, with the risk-free rate you can change), *rolling returns* (what any holding period would have earned), *the same money in the index fund* (your actual purchases put into the index fund on the same days), then value against money put in with the time-range switch, money put in each month, value by person and "If you carry on like this". Mutual funds have no maturity value, so the page shows a projection and says so. Hover, touch or the arrow keys read out the figures, and every graph has "Show the figures as a table".
- **Funds** (Investments): press **Edit** on a fund for a side panel with the few things people change (units held, amount invested, SIP amount and whether it is stopped, start date, goal, name, sold or closed; plan and exit load under More details). It says what changed, explains a wrong entry in words and shows "Saved" at once. **Add a fund** and **Add a transaction** open the same kind of panel. *Group by* person, goal, kind of fund or fund house; the goal view adds a goal-by-goal summary and puts funds with no goal under "No goal". Search, sort by any column and Export CSV work on the page.
- **Transactions** (Investments): every line of every fund with its type, units, price, amount, units held after it, the fund and the person. Filter by type (SIP, one-off, sales, switches, dividends and bonus) and by calendar or financial year, search, sort and export; long ledgers show a page at a time. A line marked **est.** is an assumed instalment that is not on a statement yet.
- **Breakdown** (Investments): *By person* side by side (including the return of the last year); *Key figures* (money put in, value, gain on the units still held, XIRR, best and weakest fund overall and today, funds and folios as last four digits); the money split *by kind of fund* (large, mid, small, flexi, index, ELSS, hybrid, debt, gold, international; read from the official category once a fund is matched, otherwise guessed from the name and marked **?**), *by fund house* and *by fund*, with how concentrated it is (the largest funds' share and "spread like N equal funds"); *Direct and Regular plans*; *how old the units are*; *gain or loss by fund* as bars either side of zero; *how much is long-term already* (equity-oriented funds only, using the long-term period in Settings, Tax rules, from the real purchases or, marked estimated, from the SIP plan and published prices, with the date the next units turn long-term); *monthly investments (SIP)* (total each month, running and stopped, paid so far, longest running, next instalment); and *gains already booked by selling* split short and long term by financial year (gains only: the tax is on Tax filing papers).
- **A fund's own page** (open a fund from Investments) has a headline row (value, invested, profit or loss, XIRR, compound growth or gains booked, SIP) and five tabs. *Overview*: the figures and graphs (the price with a dot at each purchase and your average cost line, value against money put in, units over time, money put in each month, a month-by-month SIP calendar and how much is long-term; the range switch applies). *Returns*: the fund's own price return over each period against the benchmark, growth of 100, the month grid, fall from the highest price, risk and rolling returns. The category average and the expense ratio are not shown: the free price data does not carry them. *Transactions*: every line with units, price, amount, units held, the cost and value of the units held after it, and for a purchase what it is worth today; filters, search, sort and export. *Lots and gains*: each purchase still held with its age, cost, value, gain and the date it turns long-term; each sale with the gain it booked and whether it was short or long term; the SIP calendar. *Records*: details, the values you recorded and the activity log. Edit this fund opens the side panel.
- The numbers behind these pages (how many months of history before an annual figure is drawn, how many funds make "concentration", how many months the calendar and monthly bars show, how many slices a ring has) are in the saved configuration with built-in defaults; the long-term period is the equity holding period of Tax rules.
- **Tax filing papers** lists every fund sale of the year, short and long term, with the estimated tax, and downloads the schedule 112A rows per person. Compare the column order with the portal's current template before uploading. Units bought before 1 February 2018 need the 31 January 2018 value per unit, which the file leaves blank. Each person's FD interest can be compared with the tax shown in Form 26AS.
- **Lent, policies, property** also shows property, NPS, ULIP and foreign-currency balances with the exchange rate used.
- The daily summary now also carries the tax-harvest deadline and amounts, refunds still to claim, and on the first of each month a month-in-review line per person.

## Fund statements from Gmail

**Import > Fund statement** has a card, "Read my fund statements from Gmail". Once a day the app looks through your whole Gmail (not just one label) for e-mails from CAMS, KFintech, NSDL, CDSL and MF Central that carry a PDF, opens each with the PAN saved in Settings and imports it with the same rules as an uploaded statement. **No AI is used**, so it costs nothing, and the app never sends, deletes, labels, moves or marks anything in your Gmail.

- **What it shows:** the mailbox (masked, for example `a***@g***.com`), whether it is on, the senders it accepts, the last scan ("Last scan 27 Oct 2026, 08:40: 2 statements found, 2 imported, 0 problems"), and the statements it read: date, who sent it, whose it is, and one sentence ("Fund statement from CAMS: 3 funds, 12 transactions added" or "Could not open: none of the saved passwords fits…"). What each import changed is under **Recent statements** on the same tab.
- **The first scan** looks back 400 days, so last year's statements arrive together, oldest first; later scans look back 35 days. A message already read is skipped, so running twice changes nothing. All the numbers, the list of senders and the on/off switch are in **Settings > Money rules > Gmail statements**.
- **Scan now** leaves a request for the job. The app cannot start a GitHub job by itself, so the request is picked up the next time the job starts, which is every 6 hours (at 0:40, 6:40, 12:40 and 18:40 UTC), and the card says so. - **Which statements are accepted:** only e-mails whose real sender address belongs to a listed domain (the name shown in the From line is never trusted, and `camsonline.com.example` is not `camsonline.com`), whose PDF opens with a saved password and is recognised as a fund statement. - **A family member's statements** arrive in their own mailbox. Forward the e-mail (a normal forward, with the PDF attached) to the Gmail the app reads from an address listed under Settings > Bank e-mail forwarding; it is read the same way, judged by the original sender written inside the forward.

## The overview at the top of Accounts

The panel above the bank list (on Banks and on Manage) answers "how much is in the banks and how is it doing?". ## Rates, and where to put new money

**Rates** is only about rates. Each bank has a card: its best savings rate and best NRE deposit rate, the savings rate by balance drawn as steps, how the rates last moved, and a **Where from** line that says whether each rate was typed or pasted by you or read from the bank's page (which page, which date). Show _Banks I use_ or _All banks on file_, and sort by highest deposit, highest savings, name or oldest check; the choice is in the address. Rates are read once a month, on the day set in Settings (the 30th unless you change it; the last day of a shorter month), and any time you press **Sync rates**; if it could not read a bank's page, the card says why (no AI key switched on, the bank refused, the address has changed, the page needs a browser) and what to do.

Open a bank to change its rates in place: edit the figure and press Save. If you change a figure the bank page gave, pick why it was wrong; the refresh checks the page again next time, takes the rate back when the page agrees with you, and otherwise saves a rule (shown under _How it is read_, with an on and off switch, and you can write your own). _History and log_ shows the best rates over time and every change with the old and new figure, who made it and why. Paste your notes or add a rate under _Add or import_.

**New money** answers one question: where should this amount go? Type only the amount (25 lakh or 2500000). The app looks at the savings rates and the fixed-deposit rates of every term on file, after tax (NRE interest is not taxed in India; NRO is, at the rate in Settings), and gives **five options, each with its reasons, and one recommendation**:

- **Highest return**: the most interest whatever the term, inside insurance. It ignores how the money is spread, so it is never the recommendation.
- **Balanced**: good rates, no bank above a set share, part kept in savings.
- **Ladder**: the deposit part split over several terms (6, 12, 24, 36 months by default), so money keeps coming free.
- **Safest spread**: as many banks as it takes, none above a smaller share.
- **Stay flexible**: half in savings and the rest in short deposits.

Every option stays inside the deposit-insurance limit at each bank for the chosen name, leaving a buffer for the interest that will be added, and counts what is already there (savings and deposits of the bank together, joint accounts under their names in order, foreign banks left out). The recommendation is the option that earns most among those that meet every check: inside the limit, spread over banks, part free to withdraw any time, all of the amount placed. Each option shows its lines (bank, product and term, rate, amount, interest a year, room left), what it earns over plain savings, the largest bank share, how much is free any time and within a year, and its cautions (stale rates, money that does not fit). If no fixed-deposit rates are on file the page says so at the top, since a savings-only plan understates what the money could earn. Whose money and NRE or NRO are links under the amount; each person has their own insurance room at each bank. All the numbers (longest terms, ladder terms, bank shares, the share kept in savings, the insurance buffer, the smallest deposit) are in Settings → Rates. The other tabs show which of your holdings could earn more elsewhere and, under **Insurance room**, each ownership name at each bank against the limit.

**Sync bank rates.** The **Sync rates** button on the Rates page opens **Sync bank rates** (`/rates/sync`). Every bank listed under Settings → Bank rate pages is a card there with the bank's own pages it is read from (host only, linking out), the date and plain result of the last sync, how many rates are on file and how old the newest check is, and a status chip: **Synced today**, **Unchanged**, **Cannot read** or **Not synced yet**. **Sync all banks** reads the banks one at a time with a progress bar ("Syncing 3 of 8: HDFC Bank…") and a **Stop** button between banks; each card also has its own **Sync** button and result line (for example "14 rates saved: 2 new, 3 changed, 9 unchanged", "Unchanged since 2026-10-20: not read again; 11 rates confirmed as checked today" or "Cannot read: …"). The sync needs one of your AI keys switched on; if none is, the page says so and links to Settings → Connections. It is the same reading the Monday job does, so the two never differ. A bank may have several pages, for example savings on one and fixed deposits on another: **Add another page for this bank** and say whether it carries Savings, Fixed deposits or Both. Each page is labelled with its kind, so the reader takes only savings slabs from a savings page and only deposit terms from a deposit page.

**Rates are only ever read from the bank's own pages.** The program reads the page address saved for the bank in Settings and the bank's own published pages and PDFs that it already knows (HDFC, IDFC FIRST, AU, Equitas, Yes Bank, ESAF, IndusInd, RBL). It never reads a comparison or aggregator website. Some banks publish the rate PDF behind a button or a link whose address changes with each revision (Equitas' "Interest Rates" button, the rows of IDFC FIRST's rates page): the program finds that link in the bank's own page every time, follows it only if it stays on the bank's own domain, and shows the document it found on the card ("Rates document: …"). If the link is not in the page's text because a script builds it, the card says so; no script is run.

**When a bank cannot be read.** Some banks refuse a program ("Access Denied", "Request Rejected", HTTP 403 or 503) or build their tables with a script. That is not worked around: the program does not pretend to be a browser, and no other website is tried. The card says plainly, for example "Cannot read: ESAF Small Finance Bank's site refused the automated visit (403 Access Denied)", the rates already on file stay, and **Paste the page** opens on the card: open the bank's page in your own browser, **expand every section** (collapsed sections are not copied), select all (Ctrl+A, or Cmd+A on a Mac), copy, paste, and choose whether the text has savings rates, fixed deposit rates or both. The AI reads the rates from the text and saves them marked "pasted by you"; a rate you typed is never overwritten. The monthly job runs from GitHub and the Sync page from Vercel, so a bank may answer one and refuse the other.

**Rates once saved from comparison websites.** Earlier versions could save rates from comparison websites, marked **Reference only (third party)**. They are not deleted silently. The Sync page shows how many there are ("N saved rates came from comparison websites (third party). They are kept until the bank's own page is synced, then replaced."), a bank's own sync replaces them, and **Remove rates from comparison websites** (with a confirm step) deletes exactly those rows and nothing else.

**Check sync (Settings → Bank rate pages).** Every listed bank has a **Check sync** button. It reads the saved page (and the bank's own published pages and sheets), asks your AI keys (in their order) to find the rates in it, and tells you step by step whether the monthly refresh will work: the page read, the number of characters, and how many fixed-deposit and savings rates were found. Nothing is saved. The check runs from the app's server, while the monthly refresh runs from GitHub, so a page can behave differently in the two places; if the check passes and the monthly run still fails, say so and the page is looked at again.

## Tasks

**Tasks** are grouped as overdue, due in the next 7 days, later and no date. Tick the circle when a task is done; _Not needed_ drops it. Each task links to the account, bank or page it concerns. Finished and dropped tasks are on the second tab, with a way back.

## Rates from banks that block automatic reading

Every bank with a rate page saved in Settings is read, including banks where you hold nothing yet (so you can compare before you move money), from the bank's own pages only (see **Sync bank rates** above). If nothing can be read, the reason is shown in plain words beside the bank on the Sync page and in Settings, the rates already on file stay, and you can paste the page text instead or type the rates on the Rates page. The program does not pretend to be a browser and does not get around a block.

**A page that loads but shows no rates.** Some banks serve a page whose table is built by a script, so the text a program sees has no rates. The card then says "Cannot read: the bank's page has no rates in its text (its table is built by a script)". Nothing else is tried: point Settings at a plain rate table or PDF on the bank's own site, or paste the page. **Check sync** says the same in its steps. A source note longer than the database column is shortened instead of failing the bank.

**The most you will put with one bank.** Settings > Money rules > Rates has **The most I will put with one bank** (₹1 crore to begin with). Savings slabs and deposit rates that apply only above it are not read from bank pages, are not stored and are left out of every comparison, so a rate you could never use does not appear as the best rate. A slab that reaches into the range below the limit (for example ₹50 lakh to ₹5 crore) is kept.


## Savings interest from the Rates list, and recently added deposits

- A savings account with no rate of its own is estimated at the rate on the **Rates** list for its bank, its kind (NRE or NRO) and the slab its balance falls in. The flat figure in Settings is used only when the list has nothing for that bank.
- **Recently added deposits** are their own source on the "I need money" page: a deposit booked within the number of days set in Settings (default 90) can be broken with little interest lost, so it ranks right after deposits that mature in time. You can change its priority or switch it off in Settings > Where money may come from.
- **Gifts**: on Others > Money lent, choose *Gift (will not come back, not counted)*, or mark an existing loan as a gift. It stays on the list for the record but is left out of net worth, totals, reminders and the monthly update.


## Several AI keys, in order

In **Settings > Connections > Bank e-mails and AI** the **AI keys** list holds one key per service (Anthropic, OpenRouter, Groq, Google Gemini). Each key has:

- **a place in the order.** The first key that is switched on is the **First choice**; the next ones are **Fallback 2**, **Fallback 3**. Change the order with **Move up** and **Move down** (they work with the keyboard too).
- **an On / Off switch.** A key switched off is kept but never used. Switch it back on when you want it again.
- **a status**: when it last worked (and with which model), or why and when it last failed in plain words (for example "Groq answered 429 (busy, or its limit is used up for now)"), and when it was last tested.

**How the fail-over works.** Everything that asks the AI (reading unfamiliar bank e-mails and PDF statements, reading bank rate pages, **Check sync** and **Paste the page instead** in Settings, the self-test) asks the first key. If it fails, because the service refused the key, has no credit, is busy, did not answer in time, could not be reached, or gave an answer that could not be used, the next key that is on is asked, and so on; the first usable answer is taken. Inside one service the model fallback still applies (OpenRouter tries its free models one after another). During one job run a key that was **refused or out of credit is skipped** for the rest of the run, so every bank page does not wait for the same failure; a key that was only busy or slow is tried again on the next item (and left out after three failures in a row). If no key can answer, the message lists every key's reason with what to do. The result says which key answered: the Check sync steps and the "Saved" message after pasting a page say, for example, "Groq answered after Anthropic answered 402 (no credit left)", and the inbox log and the monthly rates line say the same.

**Buttons.** **Test this key** tests that key alone (even if it is switched off) and says so. **Test the whole chain** asks the keys that are on, in order, with a made-up bank alert, exactly as a job would, and says which key answers and what each earlier key did; keys after the answering one are not asked. **Replace the key** swaps a key for a new one and keeps its place. **Remove** (it asks first) deletes it from the server. **Add a key** offers only the services that have no key yet. A key is write-only: it is never shown again after saving.

**What it costs.** Nothing extra: the free services stay free, and a pay-per-use key is only asked when the keys before it fail. If you keep Anthropic first, it is used for every reading; put a free key first if you would rather spend nothing and keep Anthropic as the fallback.

The key you had before this was added became the **First choice** automatically. The Gmail label is saved separately, under the keys.

## AI cost

**More → AI cost** shows what the AI has cost so far. Every call the app makes to an AI service (reading a bank e-mail or statement, refreshing bank rates, checking a rate page, reading a tax file, the key tests) is logged with the service, the model, what it was for and the tokens it used: new input, input read from the cache, cache writes and output. Never the text that was sent, and never a key.

- **Top row:** today, the last 7 and 30 days, this month and so far, in dollars and rupees, with the number of calls and tokens.
- **By day**, **by what it was for**, **by model**, and **recent calls** one by one with their cost and result (failed calls are marked; a call that failed costs nothing unless the service answered).
- **Prompt caching:** how much of the prompt came from the cache and what that saved.
- **Prices:** cost is worked out when the page is shown, from **Settings → Money rules → AI prices**: one line per model, `model = input / output / cache read / cache write` in dollars per million tokens, and the rupees per dollar. Change a price and the whole history is re-priced. Claude Haiku 5.5 ($0.10 in, $0.50 out) and OpenRouter's free models ($0) are set to begin with; a model with no line (Groq, Gemini) is counted in tokens but not in money until you add its line. Check the service's price page: the provider's invoice is the final word.

**Caching, and what really saves money.** Anthropic calls are marked so that the fixed instructions at the start of a request (the same for every bank and every e-mail) can be read from the cache at a tenth of the input price when two requests come within a few minutes. The page text and the e-mail change every time and cannot be cached. With Haiku this is worth fractions of a cent; the bigger saving is that a bank rate page whose text, corrections and ceiling are unchanged since it was last read is not sent to the AI at all: its rates are confirmed as checked today ("unchanged since …: not read again (no AI cost)" in the rates log), and it is read again anyway after half the time a rate counts as fresh. Long bank pages are also cut to the parts that hold the rates before they are sent.

## AI key: OpenRouter with automatic fallback

Add **OpenRouter** under **AI keys** and paste a free key from openrouter.ai/keys (the key is write-only). The reader asks OpenRouter for its free text models, orders them (large instruction models first, reasoning models last) and tries them one after another: if one is busy, rate limited, withdrawn or does not answer in the expected form, the next one is used, up to six per request. Groq and Google Gemini still work as before. To check the chain at any time, run **Check email and AI reading** in GitHub > Actions: it reads back a made-up alert and lists the models in the order they will be tried. Free models have daily limits; adding a small credit on openrouter.ai raises them.

**Testing the AI connection.** Each key in Settings > Connections shows when it was last tested and how it went. **Test this key** asks that service to read a made-up bank alert and says what worked or what to fix (for example a key with no free access). The key is used once on the server for the test and is never shown. The same check runs in GitHub > Actions > *Check email and AI reading*, which tests every key that is switched on (each on its own, so a broken fallback shows too) and also checks the Gmail label.


## Reports

**Reports** (What we hold) shows summaries laid out like a sheet, each with a grand total, a group-by switch and a **Download CSV** button. *Deposits*: the maturity summary (what matures when, by date, month, quarter, financial year or year), the start summary (what was booked when), deposits by bank, person, NRE/NRO, rate or term, and interest by financial year per person. *Mutual funds*: funds by fund, person, kind, fund house, goal (a total per goal, funds split between goals together, and "No goal" last) or start date, the SIP summary, money in and out by month or year, and how long the units have been held. The person chips narrow every report to one person. Cash held by another person appears under **Other holdings** on the Accounts page, after the banks.

**Deposit numbers are shown in full.** The deposit (FD) number is kept whole and shown on the Accounts page, the Monthly update and each deposit's page; account numbers and PANs stay masked to the last four digits. Import your workbook again (Import, "Update order, names and links") and the numbers written as *FD Number : …* in your sheet are filled in for the deposits that already exist. To change one, press Edit on the deposit and type the number under Deposit number.
